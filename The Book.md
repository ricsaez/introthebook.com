A language is made of different **types** of things. To refer to something of a given type, we want to mention it without choosing a particular **value** of that thing. To achieve this we use a **placeholder**.

For example, take the language of arithmetic. We can use `a` and `b` as placeholders for the type of things we'd call numbers. We can use the placeholders to say:

    a + b

Then choose the values of `2` for `a` and `3` for `b`:

    2 + 3

When we express something, we call what we express an **expression**.

To refer to an expression without choosing a particular one, we use the placeholder:

    e
